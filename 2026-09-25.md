**Project:** AI-Powered Pre-Merge Bug Risk Predictor  

---

## Summary

This log documents the systematic methodological audit, leakage remediation, representation engineering, and evaluation refinement carried out across the project. The work progressed logically from verifying baseline dataset validity (eliminating right-censoring bias) and eliminating cross-repository data leakage in the Leave-One-Repository-Out (LORO) evaluation pipeline to dissecting feature-family behaviors across temporal and cross-repo splits. Identifying that raw historical churn scales fail to generalize across repositories led to evaluating normalized churn representations (where Z-score standardization improved cross-repo ROC-AUC from $0.605$ to $0.657$). Subsequent threshold sensitivity and class-balancing experiments revealed that cross-repository performance is primarily constrained by domain-level label-prevalence mismatches (e.g., Flask's 11.6% baseline vs. NumPy/Django's 38.0%) rather than arbitrary decision thresholds or uniform training imbalance.

## 1. Right-Censoring Fix

**Run at:** Verified prior to dataset freeze / validated in test suite (`tests/test_censoring.py`)

### What Was Changed / Tested
PRs merged within 30 days of data collection were previously at risk of being treated as negative examples (`label=0`) simply because their 30-day observation window had not yet elapsed. We enforced a strict lookahead filter (`is_lookahead_complete`) that drops incomplete PRs from the dataset instead of assuming negative labels.

## 2. LORO Evaluation Leakage Fix

**Run at:** 2026-09-09 05:38 UTC

The previous LORO script was missing training-fold class balancing and had potential exposure to target-fold label distribution. However, mean ROC-AUC remained at $\approx 0.605\text{–}0.624$, showing that weighting adjusted the decision operating point rather than improving cross-repo ranking discrimination.

## 7. Open Questions & Next Steps

1. **Target-Domain Base-Rate Calibration:**
   Because repository positive rates vary from $11.6\%$ (Flask) to $38.0\%$ (NumPy/Django), models trained on pooled data over-predict positives on low-prevalence targets. A test-time prior adjustment (e.g., adjusting prediction logits by $\log \frac{P_{\text{target}}(y=1)}{P_{\text{train}}(y=1)}$ when repo prevalence is estimated) should be evaluated.
2. **Classifier Family Comparisons:**
   With feature representation (Z-score churn) and leakage controls stabilized, compare Gradient Boosting against calibrated Logistic Regression, Random Forests, and LightGBM to test if alternative decision surface geometries transfer better across codebases.
3. **Cross-Repo Temporal Boundary Enforcement:**
   In the current LORO setup, PRs across the 3 training repos are pooled without time-alignment across release schedules. Testing temporal cutoff alignment across training folds will determine whether temporal sequencing reduces cross-repo variance.
4. **Selective Feature Pruning for Transfer:**
   Based on the ablation study showing `file-type` features degrade LORO ROC-AUC, test a pruned model configuration combining Z-score churn, size metrics, and prior bugfix history while omitting repo-specific structural indicators.
